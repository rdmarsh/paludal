## Thinking in Paludal

**Decided:** to learn the units, people are given **landmarks**: round Paludal values tied to things they
already know the feel of, with no conversion beside them. Exact conversions are kept to their own tables
(Everyday reference, Imperial conversions and the grids in Part 2), and only the very first introduction
(the five-minute guide) compares sizes with metric.

**Why:** people learning a new system try to convert back to the old one, and with Paludal that means two
steps at once: a new base and new sizes. Converting 30 °t means reading it as decimal and then as Celsius,
and most people give up partway. Metrication shows the way out: people learned Celsius from weather
forecasts, knowing 30 is hot long before they could convert it. Countries that changed quickly and
dropped dual labels (Australia in the 1970s) moved over faster than those that kept both for decades (the
UK), where the familiar number stayed the one people read.

**Advantage:** each value means something on its own: 30 °t is warm, 4 p/br is a walk, 2 li is a newborn
baby. Nobody has to do arithmetic to read a sign or a label.

The tables below are those landmarks. Each one is a round number, so it's easy to remember, and each step
up the ladder is something you've felt or seen.
